# Resolving disagreement

Four stages. Each one escalates only when the previous one has genuinely failed, because a process
that starts at the top teaches people to start at the top.

## 1. Direct

The people who disagree discuss it in the issue or pull request where it came up.

Most disagreements end here, and most of the ones that do not end here should have. Before
escalating, check whether you are arguing about the same thing.

## 2. Facilitated

Any **uninvolved** maintainer writes a summary of both positions and proposes a resolution.

The written summary is the useful part, and it is useful more often than the proposal is. Most
technical disagreements turn out to be two people describing different problems in the same words —
and neither of them can see that from inside the argument. Someone outside it writing both
positions down is frequently the whole fix.

Uninvolved matters. A facilitator who has already argued a side is a participant with a title.

## 3. Council vote

Per the thresholds in [`council.md`](council.md), with **both positions recorded in the minutes** —
not only the one that won.

A minute that records only the outcome tells a future reader what was decided. A minute that records
the argument tells them why, which is the thing they will actually need when the question comes
back.

A tie fails, and the proposal can be brought again with a better argument. See `council.md`.

## 4. Charter test

If the disagreement is about **whether something violates the charter**, the License & Boundary
Auditor rules, and that ruling stands.

**It is not put to a vote.** The charter is not subject to majority opinion — that is what it is
for. A charter a majority can reinterpret is a preference, and this project has repeatedly chosen to
have a charter instead.

This stage does not produce a compromise. It produces an answer, and the answer may be that the
thing everybody wanted to do cannot be done.

---

## Code of conduct

**Code-of-conduct matters skip all four stages** and go directly to the process in
[`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](../../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

They are not technical disagreements and treating them as though they were — asking someone to
"discuss it directly" with a person who mistreated them, or putting their experience to a vote —
causes the harm a second time.

---

## What this process cannot do

Some disagreements are about values rather than facts, and four stages of escalation will not
resolve one. When that is what is happening, the honest outcome is to say so, record it, and let
the project's direction be decided by the people accountable for it rather than pretending a
procedure settled it.

A conflict process that claims to resolve everything is a process that will eventually be used to
apply a veneer of legitimacy to a decision somebody was going to make anyway.
